Enjoy:  When you're trying to drive real ROI with organic traffic (especially video-focused platforms like YouTube and TikTok), you need to put yourself in the shoes of the platform.

With something like paid Facebook ads, the "win-win" is pretty clear: You pay them for their advertising space, and in return they show your content to your potential customers. They spend billions of $ a year aggregating data, so you can have the absolute best results possible. It is called an "auction" for a reason – the more you spend, the more they'll distribute your content.  But what about organic platforms that you don't pay for? What are you going to give these platforms in return for showing customers your content?  Well, think to the example above – these platforms get paid through advertisments. The more advertising revenue they can make, the better.   So, if you're not going to pay them directly, you need to help them generate as much revenue as possible from people who ARE paying them.  This is where the idea of "retention" comes in. If your organic content makes viewers stay on the platform 2x as long, that's 2x more advertising space they can sell, which means it's in the PLATFORMS' best interest to show your content to more people.   This is why the meta on YouTube right now is linking other videos on your channel in your video description.  Think back to when everyone started only posting 10+ minute videos. Some people were adding blank space to the end just to get it over the 10min mark. There was a direct ROI in ad revenue.   YouTube is the strongest example of this but it goes for other platforms. This is why TikTok creators will do "Part 1" of a video, cut it off, then post Part 2, Part 3 etc.   Remember you're not just "posting content' you're directly contributing to an advertising/data monopoly. You need to pay the piper    Market awareness is way up in bizop. CPCs are 2-3x higher than they were a few years ago. Everyone is competing for the same limited space with the same angles. You can't "trick" the market anymore. Nobody is getting on a webinar without EXPECTING there to be a pitch. If you run whitehat live webinars, you'll see comments flooding with "where's the pitch course scammer!" If you're selling high awareness B2B, just drop the gimmicks altogether. Go extremely mechanistic, explain why YOUR solution is better, newer, safer, easier than your competitors. Explain what is currently happening in the world, politics, economy, technology, social trends etc. that enables your solution. Study old Agora angles, it was usually "This \[thing happening in the world] is letting people like you achieve \[outcome]" If you're selling a high ticket sales program, it's unlikely you're getting a flood of people who have NEVER heard of HTS before. So you have two options: a) Create a new angle altogether and go low awareness. Drop the HTS. There was a program (forget which one) that framed it as becoming a "digital customer service representative" where you helped potential customers understand the program, get the support they need, collect payments and onboard them into the community
b) Create a new unique mechanism that beats everything else. Why is yours fundamentally different than the competition? Why did the programs they already purchase not work for them?\
Market awareness part 2.    If selling B2C or 0-1 bizop, again just assume most of your buyers have gone through some sort of make money online funnel and don't try to trick them.    The main focus here is congruency and HONESTY.   The reason everyone's moving away from opt ins on BAC funnels is the same reason as the last tweet – they know you're not giving them a "free training" that "reveals" their secret. You're just collecting their email, sending them to a VSL and hammering them with emails.   Even if they don't know this directly from experience, they have suspision you're selling them something.   If you want them to book a call, let them book a call. Just put the calendar right underneath the VSL. Or have a button that just says "Click Here To Apply ->" that takes them right to the app.\
The best "retargetting" you can do is just being the person they want to buy from. This means having a brand. Whether that's with a big organic following, or a shit ton of retargetting ads When you're doing a typical opt-in -> book a call -> email follow ups to get them to book, all you're REALLY doing is getting the people who were 70% of the way to booking but didn't over the edge.

If someone was NOT interested in booking (or purchasing) because you fundamentally didn't get them to trust you, or explain why your solution was right for them well enough, a 5 day email sequence is NOT going to suddenly shift their entire belief structure.

Sales cycles are long. It's a barbell theory – either people are already mentally bought in and need follow ups to just take the action, or they're NOT bought in and require a LOT more time spent with you (content)

Do you think people are buying from Brez because he has an extremely dialled follow-up sequence, or because he's fucking EVERYWHERE and is top of mind 24/7 for his target audience?\
Follow up to the above: you can run retargetting ads using your best performing organic content for DIRT cheap. Facebook knows who to show it to (obviously) and they likely just need to trust you more, so don't just hammer them with sales copy but actually NURTURE them\


SaaS rapid fire for @Finityyz
(Disclaimer: I don't have direct experience with SaaS GTM strategy; this is just my opinion as a marketer)

```
                  (Also this likely only applies to B2B not consumer apps etc.)
                  
                  If you're bootstrapped, I cannot think of any valid reason for NOT drytesting demand. It's common practice in basically every other industry. I think a lot of SaaS guys unfortunately get caught up in the ego of being a "founder" and want to invent the next Cursor. So they'd actually rather burn money on making a software that looks and sounds good that a16z would like, instead of building something customers ACTUALLY want.
                  
                  MVPs can be EXTREMELY rudimentary as long as you're up front and ethical. I know a pretty big SaaS that's a B2B lead finder for a specific vertical, and their MVP was literally just a Google Sheets. Customers aren't buying fancy UI and AI automations, they're buying a collection of verified leads they can sell to. That's what matters. So they built THAT and ran traffic to it to see if people would even want the full thing.
                  
                  Also, I know there's a difference between Software and SaaS, but I personally think every software should start as a SaaS, which should start as a service. Unless you truly have a golden opportunity and are optimizing purely for funding (which is dumb). Create a service that helps a niche, and then automate it until it's a self-sustaining software. 
                  
                  The goal should be staying power. ClickFunnels is ClickFunnels because most of the big info businesses you see out there wouldn't have been born or survived without it. They'll pay $99/mo until the end of time, or until for some reason it makes more sense to go fully self-hosted
                  
                  Becker has a LOT of truly high level game on his business channel about how he scaled Hyros. Find a group of 10 of your ideal customers, and work with them for free until your product genuinely solves their problem better than anything else on the market. Then get a cash injection by giving them a really good referral/affiliate deal and start running ads.
                  
                  Becker also says his customer support team was the biggest lever he pulled for success. Every customer had onboarding reps etc, assume they were also trying to close on year-long deals. GoHighLevel sends me fucking daily emails asking if I want to get on a free Zoom meeting with a CSM
                  
                  Also, if your goal is purely cash flow + equity, don't compete with cracked MIT grads. Go replace ancient systems in legacy industries. If I were getting into SaaS I'd be building a lead finder + nurture software for insurance agents or some shit, not fucking AI powered insert 17 year old zoomer bizop.
                  
                  Also, assuming you're smart and are focusing on finding PMF before "building" anything sophisticated, I'd probably sign on with a really good GTM agency. Fight to keep equity like your life depends on it, pay heavy up front if you had to. If I took on $300k in funding I'd give $160k of it to a cracked dev with an adderall addiction and give the rest to GTM sharks that will get my MVP out no matter what.
                  
                  Overall I think "building for equity" is a dumb idea. I know we all looked at the Figma IPO and wanted to build a shiny VC backed unicorn, but if you're ever actually going to get seroius funding / exit, it's easier to build a ruthlessly effective solution that a large customer base depends on, then pay a branding agency to package it up to look pretty for investors
```
