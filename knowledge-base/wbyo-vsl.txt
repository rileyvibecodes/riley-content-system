So, What Actually Is An Offer? And Why Does Everyone Get It Wrong?
Most people think an offer is "what you're selling."
It's not.
An offer is what they're buying.
And people don't buy features. They buy benefits. They buy outcomes. They buy transformations.
When someone spends $60 on face cream, they're not buying face cream.
They're buying clear skin.
But they're not buying clear skin.
They're buying the confidence when they walk into a room.
But they're not buying confidence.
They're buying showing up to Thursday night drinks and making her "friends" jealous. She's buying the confidence she never had in high school because of her acne.
That's the offer.
The offer isn't "face cream." The offer is: "Click this button, enter your address, and in 30 days you will have the skin you wish you had when you were younger."
Now this skin cream isn't just skin cream. It's anti-acne skin cream. It's skin cream for people who struggled with acne their whole life.
Do you see how this shapes everything?
The ads aren't just Cover Girl-style glam shots. They're deep. They hit pain points. They bring her back to her high school years. The music, the vintage camera effects, the way people are dressed, the words they say, how the ad makes her FEEL.
See how that ALL starts from the offer?
The same goes for coaching, consulting, agency services, you name it.
You're not selling what you do. You're selling what they get. The transformation. The outcome.
Your offer needs to explain their current situation better than they even understand it themselves, and provide a solution that is SO obvious, that makes so much sense, that you don't need sleazy sales tactics or super slick copywriting.
You simply need to explain the offer to them.
This is how every great business operates.
Their "marketing" is just telling you about the offer.
Their "sales" is just someone walking you through the process, asking questions, bridging to answer those questions, and just making sure you understand the offer.
Because if the offer is so good, once they understand it, they'll sell themselves.

The Solution: Validation, Not Imitation
So how do you fix it?
You stop guessing. You stop imitating. And you start validating.
Here's the process:
________________


Step 1: Go To The Source
If you want to know what your customer actually wants, you have to ask them.
Not in a survey. Not in a Facebook group. Not by reading Reddit threads.
You have to listen to real conversations.
If you have existing customers, you need to analyze your sales call recordings. At least 10 of them. 5 closed deals, 5 lost deals.
You need to listen to:
* How they describe their problem (in their own words)
* What they've tried before (and why it didn't work)
* What they're actually buying (the transformation, not the mechanism)
* What their objections are (and what's really behind them)
If you don't have customers yet, you need to conduct interviews. At least 10 of them. With people who fit your target niche.
You need to ask open-ended questions and LISTEN.
Don't pitch. Don't explain. Just listen.
This is the only way to build a model of what they actually want.
________________


Step 2: Identify The Real Transformation
Once you have the data, you need to figure out what they're actually buying.
This is NOT the mechanism. This is the outcome.
Use the "5 Whys" method to dimensionalize the transformation.
Example:
* "I want more leads."
* Why? "To close more deals."
* Why? "To hit my revenue target."
* Why? "To feel secure."
* Why? "To not worry about payroll."
Now you know what they're actually buying: Security. Peace of mind. Freedom from financial stress.
That's the transformation.
Not "10 leads per week." Not "a proven lead generation system."
The transformation is: "Click this button, and in 30 days you'll stop worrying about whether you can make payroll."
They are going FROM shooting into the dark, hoping one of their ad campaigns works so they don’t need to float expenses again, TO a predictable acquisition channel that they KNOW will bring in X amount of deals per month.
That's what they're buying.
________________


Step 3: Build The Offer Around The Transformation
Once you know the transformation, the rest is easy.
You just need to:
1. Articulate their current state (better than they can articulate it themselves)
2. Show them the desired state (the transformation)
3. Present the mechanism (how you'll get them there)
4. Price it relative to the value (not relative to your costs)
When you do this, something incredible happens:
Your offer stops being "a service" and becomes a solution.
Your marketing stops being "selling" and becomes educating.
Your sales calls stop being "convincing" and become enrolling.
You stop competing on price, because your offer is in a category of one.
You stop getting ghosted, because the value is obvious.
You start attracting the right clients, who are happy to pay you what you're worth.
